Add lesson 5: handout, slides, quiz and exercise

Completes lesson 5, Friday 23 October 2026 — the lesson the syllabus calls the
centre of the course.

Handout, 19 pages: the bias-variance decomposition derived in full with the
cross terms shown to vanish; what cross-validation estimates, why it is
slightly pessimistic, and why the usual standard error is optimistic because the
folds share three quarters of their rows; a section on non-independent rows,
GroupKFold and TimeSeriesSplit; and the leakage that survives cross-validation.

Slides, 46 of them with speaker notes throughout and nine figures. Two new
conceptual diagrams: the k-fold test block rotating along the dataset, and the
two loops of nested cross-validation with the outer block marked as the only
number you may report.

Quiz, 29 questions across six parts, several of them asking the student to
criticise a described experiment rather than recall a definition.

Exercise 5, due Friday 30 October, built around a notebook that reports AUC 0.92
and is wrong in three separate ways. 480 weekly readings from 40 wind turbines,
where the installation metadata is constant per turbine and therefore acts as a
fingerprint: with a random split it scores 0.688 on its own, with GroupKFold
0.487, which is chance. Students must predict the effects before testing them,
isolate each flaw one at a time, and tell the maintenance team what the model is
actually worth.

Also expand AUC once in each lesson 5 artefact. Lesson 4 defines it thoroughly,
but the rule is per-document — a student reading only these slides has not read
that handout.
