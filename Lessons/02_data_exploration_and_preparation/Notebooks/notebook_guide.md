# Notebook 01 — `01_exploratory_data_analysis`

## Looking at a dataset before touching it

**What it is for.** To build the picture that every later decision depends on: how big
the data is, what each column means, how much is missing, where the outliers live, and
which variables move together. Nothing is fitted here — the whole dataset is still
unsplit, on purpose.

**The data.** A synthetic telecom dataset generated by `churn_data.py`: 2,000
customers, 8 columns, and the question of whether each one churned. Synthetic **so that
the ground truth is known** — which is what makes notebook 3's demonstration
conclusive.

**The concepts, in order.**

1. **`.info()` and `.describe()` first.** They surface the problems in seconds:
   `tenure_months` runs from **−3 to 999**, `monthly_charges` to **3344.74** with a
   standard deviation of 171 against a median of 63.
2. **The baseline.** Churn rate **0.194**, so always predicting "no churn" is right
   **80.6%** of the time.
3. **Missingness, with its mechanism.** `age` 160 rows (**8.0%**), missing completely at random (MCAR);
   `num_support_calls` 96 rows (**4.8%**), missing at random (MAR) — missing more often for long-tenure
   customers.
4. **What filling a gap would cost**, measured against the derivation:

   | fraction filled | correlation | kept | $\sqrt{1-p}$ |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 0.0% | 0.700 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
   | 8.0% | 0.671 | 0.959 | 0.959 |
   | 40.0% | 0.543 | 0.776 | 0.775 |

   The last two columns agree to a thousandth at every fraction: **the attenuation
   formula arriving on data.**
5. **Outliers from scratch**, both rules implemented from their formulas. On
   `monthly_charges`: z-score flags 20, Tukey flags 32, and they agree on 20.
6. **The twist that makes the section.** The contamination argument is *right about the
   damage and wrong about the consequence*: the errors inflate $s$ from **17.23 to
   171.25** and push the fence from **115 to 593** — but the smallest genuine billing
   error is **780.1**, so the ruined fence still catches all 20. Tukey catches the same
   20 plus **12 ordinary customers** (values like 15.0 and 128.4).
7. **What neither rule can see.** Six negative tenures, none flagged by the interquartile range (IQR) rule,
   because −3 sits comfortably inside a column spanning 0 to 72. The domain rule
   $0 \le \texttt{tenure} \le 120$ flags **10 rows**.
8. **Correlations and cardinality.** `tenure_months` and `num_support_calls` correlate
   with churn, `age` barely does — matching how the data was generated. And
   `zip_code` has **493 levels over 2,000 rows**, which is the fuse for notebook 3.

**The two-minute version.** *This notebook is the look before the touch. Two calls,
`.info()` and `.describe()`, already show a tenure of 999 and one of −3, and a
monthly charge of 3,344. Then the missingness: 8% of age, missing completely at random,
and 4.8% of support calls, missing because of tenure. The table of imputation costs
matches the square-root-of-one-minus-p formula to a thousandth. Then the two outlier
rules disagree — 20 against 32 — and the interesting part is that the z-score rule was
right by luck: the errors inflated its ruler fivefold, and it catches them only because
they are enormous. Finally, six negative tenures that no statistical rule flags at all,
because being impossible is not the same as being unusual.*

# Notebook 02 — `02_scaling_encoding_and_pipelines`

## Scaling, encoding, and the pipeline that holds them together

**What it is for.** To build the preprocessing that notebook 1 argued for, and to prove
the scaling half rather than assert it. It implements handout sections 5, 6 and 8.

**The concepts, in order.**

1. **Split first.** 1,500 training rows against 500; baseline 0.806. `zip_code` is
   dropped for now — it returns, deliberately, in notebook 3.
2. **Why scale, measured.** Feature standard deviations 0.98 and 10.23, a **variance
   ratio of 110**. Raw features tolerate a learning rate of 0.1 and reach a loss of
   0.4183; scaled features tolerate **2.0**, twenty times larger, and reach 0.4155 in a
   fraction of the iterations.
3. **What happens at the wrong rate.** Raw features at $\eta = 2.0$: the loss starts at
   0.6931 and then **swings between 0.69 and 8.29** — still finite after 200 steps and
   no closer to settling. No exception, no warning, no convergence.
4. **StandardScaler against MinMaxScaler**, both fitted on training data only. MinMax
   is the more fragile: one extreme billing error stretches the whole $[0,1]$ range and
   compresses every ordinary customer into a sliver of it.
5. **The dummy variable trap.** All $k$ dummies plus an intercept: **4 columns, rank
   3.** With $k-1$ dummies: 3 columns, rank 3. The redundancy the derivation predicts,
   on the actual matrix.
6. **Why `zip_code` waits.** 493 levels over 2,000 rows, **4.1 rows per level**, and
   **99.80% of the one-hot matrix is zero**.
7. **`ColumnTransformer` + `Pipeline`.** Numeric columns imputed then scaled,
   categoricals imputed then one-hot, all in one object: accuracy **0.820** against a
   baseline of 0.806, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) **0.751**
   (the chance a random churner is scored above a random non-churner; 0.5 is a coin flip). The small accuracy gain is the imbalance doing
   what lesson 1 warned it would.
8. **Feature engineering, inside the pipeline.** `total_paid` moves AUC from **0.7514
   to 0.7548**, a gain of 0.0034 — and **to 0.7439 when the domain rule is applied
   first**, a loss of 0.0075.

**The two-minute version.** *This is where the preprocessing gets built and one claim
gets proved. The two features differ in variance by a factor of 110, and that is
exactly what decides the learning rate: raw data tolerates 0.1, scaled data tolerates
2.0 and converges far sooner. Push the raw data to 2.0 and the loss oscillates between
0.69 and 8.29 forever — no error, just never converging. Then encoding: all the dummies
plus an intercept give four columns of rank three, which is the dummy trap on the real
matrix. Everything goes into one ColumnTransformer and Pipeline, AUC 0.751. The last
section adds an engineered feature and gains 0.003 — and loses 0.008 when the data is
cleaned first, because the feature had been multiplying the contamination.*

# Notebook 03 — `03_leakage_in_preprocessing`

## Two leaks that look nothing like leaking

**What it is for.** Lesson 1 broke train/test independence in the most visible way.
This notebook breaks it in the two ways that look like ordinary preprocessing.

**The concepts, in order.**

1. **Leak 1 — imputing with the whole dataset.** 128 training rows have a missing
   `age`, and **94 of the 128 had at least one test row among their five donors**. The
   notebook even names one: training row 1922 was imputed partly from test row 1127.
2. **And then the honest surprise.** Leaky AUC **0.7553**, honest AUC **0.7546** — a
   gap of seven ten-thousandths. Rerun over 20 splits and the gap **changes sign in 14
   of them**, mean −0.0003. **The gap is noise.** The contamination is real and
   measurable in the donors; its effect on this score is not measurable at all, which
   is a more honest and more useful result than a dramatic number would be.
3. **Leak 2 — encoding with the whole dataset.** `zip_code` carries no signal by
   construction. Encoded before the split: accuracy 0.846, **AUC 0.891**. Encoded
   honestly with a cross-fitted `TargetEncoder` inside the pipeline: accuracy 0.820,
   **AUC 0.751** — which is what the model scored in notebook 2 without the column at
   all, exactly as it should be.
4. **Why small groups are what make it dangerous.** The notebook finds a code with one
   customer: their churn label is 0, and their leaky encoded feature is 0.000.
   **For that row the feature is not a predictor of the label. It is the label.**

**The two-minute version.** *Two leaks that look like preprocessing. First, imputing
`age` with a k-nearest-neighbours imputer fitted before the split: 94 of the 128
affected training rows borrowed a value from a test row. And the AUC barely moves —
seven ten-thousandths, which flips sign across splits, so the leak is real and its
effect here is unmeasurable. That is worth saying plainly: the damage is in the
method, not in the number. Second, target-encoding `zip_code` before the split takes
AUC from 0.751 to 0.891 — fourteen points out of a column that contains nothing, by
construction. The mechanism is at the end: a zip code with one customer gets an encoded
value identical to that customer's label. The feature is the answer.*
